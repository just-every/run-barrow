# Buyer-approved substitutions (STK-005)

Signed-in people use `POST /api/barrow/commands` with the installation's Origin header and their native session. The body is `{ "type": "substitution.preview", "payload": { ... } }`; responses wrap the result in `result`. Agent credentials cannot use these commands. The existing account permissions apply to every original and replacement order.

1. The supplier reads `GET /api/barrow/orders/<id>` and records its `revision`. For a fully unshipped accepted or shortfall order, call `substitution.preview` with `orderId` and a **complete replacement basket**: `lines: [{fromSku:"OLD-S",fromUnit:"each",sku:"NEW-M",unit:"each",quantity:6}]`. Each line identifies its original line; include unchanged lines explicitly. Multiple new lines can refer to one original. Units, prices, minimums and account assortment come from the current account and inventory authority. The preview shows exact lines, current delivery options and an explicit tax unknown.
2. Call `substitution.propose` with the same basket, `orderRevision` and `reason`. When delivery is configured, supply `delivery` with the selected preview option's `methodId` and `warehouse`, and `ownCarrierAccount` for an own-carrier option. An approved binding freight amount uses `freightQuote` with `methodId`, `warehouse`, `cents`, `windowStart`, `windowEnd` and `reason`. The supplier's input selects a method; rates and windows are always calculated on the server. The result is the original order with a new immutable numbered entry in `substitutions`. The Orders page displays the original and replacement quantities, selling units, exact USD prices/totals, delivery and the unverified tax difference. No stock changes.
3. The account buyer reads that exact offer, then calls `substitution.decline` with `orderId`, `number` and `reason`, or `substitution.accept` with `orderId`, `number` and `acknowledged:true`. A decline preserves the offer; the supplier proposes the next numbered revision. Only the latest offer is actionable. Acceptance rechecks current account revision, assortment, prices, units, available stock and delivery. It creates one linked submitted replacement, without reserving or shipping anything. Repeated acceptance returns the same replacement ID.
4. The supplier calls `substitution.execute` with `orderId` and `number`. Barrow requests the original order's stock release with STK-004's durable identity, waits for its acknowledgment, then approves/reserves the replacement under its own durable order identity. Retry the same command after a lost response. A pending release holds its units out of every buyer's promiseable stock. The normal approval door also refuses the replacement until the original release is confirmed. Dispatch on the original refuses substitution; returns are a separate flow. A later stock shortage on replacement approval stays a visible shortfall.

Every offer keeps its proposer, reason, decision person/time and replacement ID. Original order lines, agreed prices, reservation snapshot and shortfall event remain intact. Replacement lines name their original order, line, SKU and selling unit in `substitutedFrom`; the replacement names `fromSubstitution`. JSON and order/line CSV exports preserve these links, and portability archives preserve the records. The timeline records offer, decline, acceptance and replacement events. Historical AUD orders keep their original currency and cannot be relabeled with USD replacement prices.

Tax is currently unrecorded: an offer never claims a calculated tax difference. Delivery is the existing merchant delivery quote, not a verified carrier rate. TAX-001..004 and SHIP-002..003 inherit source-confirmed tax and carrier differences before adding their capabilities. No account policy granting automatic substitution authority exists; each offer requires the account buyer's explicit acceptance. ACC-002/005/006 inherit company purchasing and supplier/rep scope when those roles ship.

# Seasonal deposit allocations

A captured seasonal deposit remains attached to its original commitment and card receipt. Releasing a ship window creates a linked order; only a fully acknowledged release with an issued invoice can receive a deposit allocation. The company, seller and currency must match the original commitment.

Open the release under Orders, expand **Credit notes and refunds**, then **Manage credits and refunds**. Select its captured seasonal receipt, review the unapplied principal, enter the exact amount and a reason, then choose **Apply card principal**. A supplier owner or admin can decide directly. A finance operator needs current account access and an explicit sufficient USD credit allowance.

Each receipt has one immutable allocation per release invoice. Repeating the same amount returns that allocation; a changed amount is refused. Allocation rechecks the connected merchant's authenticated payment object and the current commitment and release revisions. Concurrent decisions cannot exceed captured principal or unpaid invoice principal. Card surcharge never supplies allocatable principal. Refunds remain tied to the original receipt and the particular credited release's allocated principal. Pending refunds reserve that previously allocated money; their eventual failure or success does not make the original allocation spendable again.

For a native command, use `card.allocate` with `paymentId`, `orderId`, `amountCents`, `reason`, `orderRevision` and `sourceRevision`. The release order supplies `orderRevision`. Its `GET /api/barrow/credits?orderId=ORDER_ID` response supplies `depositReceipts`, including each receipt's `availableCents` and current `sourceRevision`. Refresh after a revision conflict. Agent credentials and buyers cannot allocate funds.

The account statement records the cash receipt once. Dated allocation rows explain the release settlement with no second cash effect. The page, CSV and PDF retain the allocation and provider references and show unapplied principal separately. Release invoices carry the sale; the original commitment is not another invoice. Sales and finance reports reduce the correct release balance, while current credit exposure falls only for explicitly allocated principal. Unapplied cash remains separately disclosed.

Payment-basis commissions use the original frozen merchandise prices and split. Allocated principal is distributed over original merchandise and freight cents; freight does not earn. Credits first reduce unpaid principal. Earning is capped by the remaining original merchandise basis, with any paid excess reversed once. A refund never adds another reversal for the same credited return or cancellation.

Refresh the existing next-action queue to create or reconcile the owned seasonal-deposit review. Capture alone cannot close that review. Closure requires recorded allocation and refund evidence. Financial exports retain the stable allocation, commitment, release, provider, currency and revision identities. The manifest counts each order document, while `orderCents` counts the original commitment once and excludes linked releases. `documentCents` retains the raw document sum; `commitmentCents`, `releaseCents` and `invoiceCents` explain the separate stages and must not be added together as sales. Retention follows later source events across the linked documents. Erasure preserves required financial history and fences reusable payment authority; restoring history cannot initiate another allocation or provider operation. Stripe retains separate copies; no provider deletion is claimed.

In **Settings: seasonal prebooks**, publish either a percentage in basis points or a fixed amount in the season’s minor units. The prepared commitment retains the exact requirement. A fixed deposit exceeding the commitment principal is refused; published terms remain immutable.

For an issued release, the owner or admin opens **Card payment → Schedule remaining payments** and records dated exact principal amounts totaling the available balance. Each date is a payment obligation, with no automatic charge or outreach. The buyer selects the dated payment, uses **Prepare scheduled card payment**, completes hosted card entry and separately authorizes the exact principal and surcharge. The merchant reviews and captures each payment separately. A new unscheduled partial collection uses **Collection principal in minor units**. Captured receipts still require explicit finance allocation to the original invoice.

Native clients use `card.schedule` with `orderId`, `orderRevision`, a durable `scheduleKey`, `entries` containing `dueDate` and `amountCents`, and a reason. To collect an entry, pass its `paymentId` and `orderId` to `card.prepare`. An unscheduled partial payment uses a durable `collectionKey` and `amountCents`. Replaying an identity retains its amount; changed amounts are refused. Other active collections and captured unapplied principal reserve the balance, so concurrent requests cannot collect it twice. Unused attempts can be explicitly cancelled by the merchant before a replacement plan is reviewed.

Due scheduled payments and captured unapplied partial receipts appear in the owned next-action queue. Contact notes cannot settle them; immutable allocations or an attributed cancellation provide resolution evidence. JSON and card-payment CSV retain schedule identity, dates, actor, reason and revision. Financial retention includes schedule creation; reviewed personal erasure removes schedule notes while preserving exact principal, dates and opaque provider references, and older archives reapply that erasure journal.

The complete live-provider deposit, two-release and partial-payment receipt remains unproved. Controlled provider-boundary checks establish the native flow, without establishing live provider completion or public callback delivery.
